Omniflick privacy policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

This policy explains what the Omniflick app (for Android TV, phones and tablets) does with your information. Omniflick is made by an independent developer, named as the developer on the app's Google Play listing ("we" or "the developer" below). It is free, has no ads and contains no analytics or advertising trackers.

In short
  • Your lists, ratings and settings are kept on your device.
  • If you choose to sign in with Google, they are synced to a private cloud record that only you can read.
  • Crash reports are sent so bugs can be fixed. They aren't linked to your name or email, and you can turn them off.
  • Ask AI uses your own API key, which never leaves your device except to go to the AI service you picked.
  • We don't sell your data, share it for advertising, or use it for anything other than running the app.

1. Data stored on your device

The app keeps the following in its private storage on each device. Other apps can't read it.

  • Your profiles, ratings, My List (watchlist), watched history, "Not interested" list and settings (such as your region and streaming services).
  • API keys for the film data services (TMDB and OMDb), if you enter your own.
  • Ask AI keys, if you add one (see section 3).
  • A short "Recent log" of what the app did lately (see section 4).

This data stays on the device unless you turn on sync. You can delete it at any time (see Delete your data).

2. Google sign-in and sync (optional, off by default)

You can use Omniflick without signing in. If you choose to sign in with Google to keep your data the same on all your devices:

  • The app asks Google only for your name, email address and basic profile (the openid, email and profile scopes). It keeps a sign-in on the device so you stay signed in and can see which account is in use.
  • The data in section 1 (except Ask AI keys and the Recent log) is saved to a cloud database, Google Firebase Cloud Firestore, in a record that belongs to your Google account.
  • The database's security rules let only you, when signed in, read or change your record. Other people who use the app can't see it.
  • Firebase Authentication records your email address, account ID and when you signed in, as part of its sign-in service.
  • As the owner of the Firebase project, the developer can technically view the database, but does not look at or use your data except to help you when you ask (for example, to delete it).

Synced data is used only to provide sync to you. It is not shared with anyone, used for advertising, or sold. Omniflick's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Firebase is run by Google, and data may be processed in the United States or other countries where Google operates, under Firebase's privacy and security terms.

Older test versions synced through a file in your Google Drive's hidden app folder instead. Omniflick no longer does this; see Delete your data to remove that file.

3. Ask AI (optional, off until you add a key)

Ask AI suggests films and series using an AI service you choose: Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI (ChatGPT) or Google (Gemini), with your own API key.

  • Your key stays on your device. It's stored encrypted, with a key held in the Android Keystore. It's never synced, is left out of Android backups and device transfers, and is never sent to the developer or anyone else. It's sent only to the AI service you picked. You can remove it in Settings → Ask AI.
  • Sending a key from your phone to a TV: the QR code on the TV opens a page served by the TV itself over your home Wi-Fi, not the internet. The TV accepts it only while Settings → Ask AI is open, and only at the one-time address in the QR code.
  • What's sent when you ask: your request (typed, or spoken and turned into text by Android's voice service), your country, the names of your streaming services, and a short summary of the current profile's taste: up to 60 highly rated, 25 low-rated and 40 middling titles with your scores, up to 150 other titles you've seen, up to 60 "Not interested" titles and up to 80 watchlist titles (title, year, and whether it's a film or series). No profile name, Google account, email address or device details are sent.
  • The AI service handles this under its own terms: Anthropic, OpenAI or Google. The app asks OpenAI not to store requests. Google may use requests made on the free tier of the Gemini API to improve its products.
  • Suggested titles are then looked up on TMDB, by name only, to check they exist and are on your services.

4. Crash reports and the Recent log

  • Recent log (on the device only): the app keeps its last 500 lines of activity (for example "Synced 120 titles" or "Opened Netflix") in its private storage. You can read or clear it in Settings → Recent log, and on a phone share it yourself. It never contains API keys, sign-in tokens, email addresses, names, Letterboxd usernames, what you searched for or what you asked the AI. It does contain title IDs (for example "movie:603") and the device's model and Android version.
  • Crash reports (on by default): if the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends a report to Google Firebase Crashlytics. A report holds the error and where in the app's code it happened, the device model, Android version, free memory and disk space, the app version, a random ID for this installation, and the Recent log lines described above. It isn't linked to your Google account or email address, even when you're signed in. Reports are used only to fix bugs and are kept for 90 days.
  • Turning crash reports off: Settings → Recent log → Crash reports (on each device). Turning it off also deletes any reports not yet sent.

5. Other services the app connects to

To work, the app talks directly to these services. None of these requests include your name, email address or Google account.

Like any internet request, these services can see your device's IP address. Opening a title launches the streaming app you choose, which has its own privacy policy. A Letterboxd export file you import is read on your device; on a TV, your phone sends it to the TV over your home Wi-Fi.

Voice search uses Android's own speech service. If your TV has no voice pop-up, the app asks for microphone permission and listens only while you're using voice search. The app doesn't record or keep audio.

6. Sharing and selling

We don't sell your data, share it with advertisers or data brokers, or use it to build advertising profiles. Data goes only to the services described above, which act on our behalf (Firebase) or which you choose to use (AI services, streaming apps). We would disclose data only if required by law.

7. Security

All network traffic uses encrypted connections (HTTPS), except the transfers on your own home Wi-Fi described above. Synced data is protected by Firebase's security rules so only your signed-in account can reach it. Ask AI keys are encrypted on the device.

8. How long data is kept

  • Device data: until you delete it, clear the app's data or uninstall.
  • Synced data and sign-in record: until you ask for them to be deleted.
  • Crash reports: 90 days.

9. Deleting your data

See Delete your data for step-by-step instructions, including how to ask for your synced data and sign-in record to be deleted.

10. Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask to see, correct or delete the data held about you, or to get a copy of it, by contacting us (below). If you're in the European Economic Area or the UK, we process your data to provide features you've asked for (sync and Ask AI) and, for crash reports, on the basis of our legitimate interest in fixing bugs, which you can opt out of in Settings. You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority, such as Ireland's Data Protection Commission.

11. Children

Omniflick is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. Signing in to sync requires a Google account.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new "Last updated" date. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the app's release notes.

13. Contact

For privacy questions or requests, email the developer at the address shown under "Developer contact" on Omniflick's Google Play listing, or open an issue on GitHub (please don't post personal details there).
